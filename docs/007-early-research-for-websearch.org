* Status

These are working notes from the early research and brainstorming phase.  They
record the evidence, calculations, rejected ideas, and current design direction;
they are not yet an approved implementation specification.

No code was changed during this research phase.

* Executive conclusions

- The desired scale is approximately *5,000 programmatic searches/day*, or
  approximately *150,000/month*.
- The average request rate is small: =5,000 / 86,400 = 0.0579 requests/second=.
  Per-minute and per-second limits matter for report bursts, but *monthly free
  credits are the binding constraint*.
- Approximately *114 fresh searches/day* are available from the verified
  recurring direct/AI-search free tiers (Exa, Brave, and Tavily basic).
- Including fixed-quota third-party SERP intermediaries, especially Bright
  Data, raises the documented recurring total to approximately *286/day*.
- A broad optimistic ceiling, including variable generic scraping credits, is
  approximately *330/day*, but it is not reliable planning capacity.
- One-time signup and trial grants provide approximately *25,000--31,000
  searches*, enough for only approximately *5--6 days* at the target rate.
- Self-hosted SearXNG can accept 5,000 local calls/day, but it owns no search
  index.  It fans queries out to upstream engines that can return CAPTCHAs,
  throttle, block the host IP, or change markup.  It therefore cannot guarantee
  fresh results or a 99% fresh-search SLA.
- A hard-$0 system cannot guarantee 99% fresh, unrestricted, general-web search
  at 5,000/day.  It can target *99% non-empty tool responses* only because the
  accepted success definition includes fresh cache hits, stale cache hits, and
  explicit partial results.
- The recommended baseline is a local resilient search gateway:
  =fresh cache -> healthy authorized/live engines -> stale cache -> explicit
  partial response=.
- Pi must continue to orchestrate every query and receive every result.  Hosted
  consumer research agents are not substitutes for a raw search backend.
- Quota routing must use one legitimate account per provider and stay within
  every provider's authorization.  Multi-account quota multiplication, stealth
  automation, proxy rotation, CAPTCHA solving, and similar circumvention are
  excluded.

* Mission and constraints

** Workload

- Approximately 1--10 reports/day.
- A report may fan out to approximately 1,000 searches.
- Peak daily workload may reach approximately 5,000 searches.
- Preserve high fan-out; do not redefine the problem by forcing reports to use
  only a small number of searches.
- Expected cache reuse is only approximately 10--40%.

** Hard requirements

- Maximum monthly operating cost: *$0*, including APIs, proxies, and servers.
- Self-hosting is allowed when it uses already-available hardware/connectivity.
- Pi controls query generation, dispatch, result receipt, synthesis, and report
  production.
- General-web queries must not be restricted to a small curated corpus.
- Cached, stale, or partial non-empty results count toward the availability
  objective, but their status must be explicit.
- Fresh-only 99% coverage is not required after clarification.
- Respect provider terms, hard quotas, burst limits, =Retry-After=, and blocks.

** Explicit non-goals

- No multiple accounts at one provider to multiply a free allowance.
- No rotating identities, IPs, or proxies to evade a rate limit or block.
- No browser fingerprint mimicry, stealth scraping, or CAPTCHA solving.
- No automation of consumer chat subscriptions as unofficial search APIs.
- No hidden conversion of a stale or partial result into a result labeled
  =fresh=.
- No claim that best-effort upstream scraping provides an SLA.

* Existing extension architecture

The current extension is intentionally small and provider-oriented:

- =extensions/web-search/index.ts= registers =web_lookup= and =fetch_web=.
- =extensions/web-search/search.ts= implements =webLookup()= and walks an
  ordered provider chain.  The first available provider returning results wins.
- =extensions/web-search/types.ts= contains shared search/fetch result types.
- =extensions/web-search/engines/exa.ts= calls Exa directly.
- =extensions/web-search/engines/duckduckgo.ts= scrapes DuckDuckGo's HTML
  endpoint and decodes redirect URLs.
- =extensions/web-search/engines/tavily.ts= calls Tavily directly.
- =extensions/web-search/strategies/readability.ts= fetches pages and extracts
  readable content with Mozilla Readability and linkedom.
- =tests/web-search.test.ts= contains the web-search extension tests.
- =skills/web-search/SKILL.md= provides engine-selection judgment,
  search-vs-fetch guidance, prompt-injection safety, and recovery guidance.

Current automatic chain:

1. Exa.
2. DuckDuckGo fallback.

Tavily is registered but opt-in rather than part of =engine: "auto"=.

The extension already has process-level usage caps:

- =--web-search-max-lookups=
- =--web-search-max-fetches=

Those caps prevent accidental use inside one process; they are not persistent
cross-session provider quota accounting and do not provide caching, health
tracking, or circuit breaking.

Historical compatibility note: the tool is named =web_lookup= rather than
=web_search=.  =web_search= collides with Anthropic's reserved server-side web
search tool name and previously broke requests through an Anthropic-compatible
non-Anthropic gateway.

** Baseline volume

queued.  The scarce resource is the monthly allowance.

* What 5,000 calls/day requires

With expected cache reuse:

| Cache reuse | Cache-served calls/day | Remaining live demand/day | After 286/day fixed free API capacity | Best-effort/unmetered demand |
|-------------+------------------------+---------------------------+---------------------------------------+------------------------------|
| 10% | 500 | 4,500 | 4,500 - 286 | 4,214/day |
| 40% | 2,000 | 3,000 | 3,000 - 286 | 2,714/day |

Using the safer 250/day metered budget leaves approximately 2,750--4,250 daily
queries for SearXNG, DuckDuckGo HTML, Jina, other best-effort routes, or stale
cache.

The verified fixed recurring APIs therefore cover only:

- Approximately 5.7% of all 5,000 daily calls when intermediaries are included.
- Approximately 2.3% when only Exa, Brave, and Tavily are counted.
- Approximately 6.4--9.5% of the post-cache live demand with intermediaries,
  depending on cache reuse.

* SearXNG findings

** What it provides

- A self-hosted metasearch HTTP service.
- Query fan-out to multiple configured upstream search engines.
- A normalized result shape over heterogeneous upstreams.
- Engine configuration, timeout handling, and suspension/cooldown mechanisms.
- A practical place to centralize best-effort search traffic on local hardware.

** What it does not provide

- Its own general-web crawler or independent index.
- A contractual quota from Google, Bing, DuckDuckGo, or other scraped engines.
- Immunity from CAPTCHA challenges, rate limits, datacenter-IP blocks, or
  upstream markup changes.
- A fresh-result SLA.

SearXNG documentation explicitly discusses upstream bot classification,
CAPTCHAs, and engine suspension.  Cooldowns reduce repeated failures but do not
turn an unauthorized or blocked route into authorized capacity.

If one local SearXNG query enables four upstream engines, 5,000 Pi calls can
produce approximately 20,000 upstream requests/day.  Fan-out therefore
increases breadth and failure independence but also accelerates upstream
blocking and consumes more network work than the Pi-call count suggests.

The PDF included anecdotal DuckDuckGo blocking near 50+ queries/hour on some
shared/datacenter IPs.  This is not a guaranteed threshold; the actual limit is
unpublished, dynamic, and dependent on IP reputation and traffic shape.

** Appropriate role

Treat SearXNG as *best-effort enrichment and overflow*, never as guaranteed free
fresh capacity.  Honor a block, CAPTCHA, or =Retry-After= by cooling down or
suspending the engine.  Do not add proxies or stealth techniques to force
continued access.

* Provider and product findings

** Exa

- Direct API and suitable result structure for Pi.
- $20 signup credit plus $10 recurring monthly credit were documented.
- No payment method is required for the free tier.
- Basic search was priced at $7/1,000, yielding approximately 1,428 recurring
  searches/month.
- The terms include anti-abuse/anti-circumvention and suspension provisions.
  Creating many accounts to multiply credits is therefore operationally
  fragile and contrary to the design's compliance requirement.

** Brave Search API

- Independent search API with a documented recurring credit.
- $5 monthly credit at $5/1,000 searches yields 1,000/month.
- Published capacity found during research: 50 requests/second.
- A credit card is required even for the free plan.
- Brave explicitly prohibits bypassing limits by creating multiple accounts.

** Tavily

- 1,000 monthly credits.
- Basic search consumes one credit; advanced consumes two.
- Approximately 33 basic or 16 advanced searches/day on a 30-day average.
- Development rate limit found: 100 requests/minute.
- Existing extension support is opt-in, so it can be integrated into a
  quota-aware chain without inventing a new provider implementation.

** Bright Data SERP API

- Official docs advertise 5,000 SERP API credits every month with no credit
  card.
- Billing is per successful request; failed/errored requests are not billed in
  the standard configuration.
- Custom headers/cookies change that behavior: all attempts may then be billed.
- Parsing, proxy management, retries, unlocking, and CAPTCHA handling are
  included according to its docs.
- It is the largest documented recurring free bucket found, but it is a
  vendor-managed SERP scraper rather than first-party index access.  Its API
  authorization does not automatically settle every upstream search engine's
  policy.  This is a policy/legal-risk distinction, not a claim of illegality.

** DuckDuckGo HTML

- Works today as the current no-key fallback.
- It has no published programmatic quota or SLA.
- HTML parsing can break when markup changes.
- Rate limits and blocks are dynamic; it cannot be included in guaranteed
  numeric capacity.

** Jina Search

- Keyless access and authenticated higher limits are documented.
- No stable contractual free daily/monthly request allowance was found.
- Useful only as a best-effort route until a quota is explicitly documented.

** Serpstack, Zenserp, SerpAPI, SearchAPI, Serper, and Scrapingdog

- These are third-party SERP services or trials, not independent general-web
  indexes.
- Verified recurring quantities were small: Serpstack 100/month and Zenserp
  50/month.
- SerpAPI's current free quantity was not sufficiently visible in the official
  extracted page to count conservatively.
- SearchAPI, Serper, and Scrapingdog were treated as signup/trial capacity, not
  recurring capacity.

** ScraperAPI and Apify

- Both provide legitimate APIs/platforms with recurring free resources.
- They are generic scraping infrastructure or actors, so one credit, one
  result, one SERP page, and one user query are different units.
- They can be optional overflow experiments, but the router must learn actual
  observed credit cost before assigning a hard daily budget.

* Consumer subscriptions and hosted agents

The following were considered and rejected as raw capacity sources:

- ChatGPT subscriptions.
- Gemini consumer subscriptions.
- Perplexity subscriptions.
- Claude subscriptions.
- OpenCode Go.
- Kagi and Perplexity paid APIs under a hard-$0 budget.

Reasons:

- Consumer plans provide hosted interactive research, not 5,000 raw
  programmatic search calls/day for Pi.
- Automating a consumer UI would be fragile and commonly disallowed.
- A hosted research agent would control query planning and result handling,
  violating the requirement that Pi orchestrate every query and receive every
  result.
- OpenCode Go covers model inference rather than increased web-search capacity.
- OpenCode's unauthenticated Exa MCP route has no appropriate published quota
  or SLA and is not a basis for production planning.

* Compliance and routing rules

** Allowed

- One legitimate account per independent provider.
- Weighted or quota-aware routing across providers.
- Pacing requests below each published QPS/RPM limit.
- Tracking monthly credits locally and stopping before exhaustion.
- Honoring =429=, =Retry-After=, CAPTCHA, and explicit blocks.
- Caching, in-flight deduplication, stale fallback, and partial responses.
- Using a provider's documented API according to its own terms.

** Excluded

- Waiting for one account to be blocked and continuing through another account
  at the same provider.
- Multiple accounts, identities, or organizations created to multiply a free
  tier.
- IP/proxy rotation intended to defeat an upstream limit.
- Browser fingerprint spoofing or stealth plugins for this feature.
- CAPTCHA solving or bypass.
- Describing an intermediary API as first-party authorization from the upstream
  engine.

Provider rotation increases resilience only when each route is independently
authorized.  It does not convert scraping or quota bypass into authorized use.

* Approaches considered

** Approach 1: API-only free-tier router

Use Exa, Brave, Tavily, and small documented free APIs with persistent quota
tracking.

Advantages:

- Clear result contracts.
- Easier accounting and circuit breaking.
- Better policy position than direct scraping.

Disadvantages:

- Only approximately 114/day from direct/AI-search APIs.
- Only approximately 286/day even after adding fixed SERP intermediaries.
- Cannot approach 5,000 fresh searches/day.

Conclusion: useful as the high-quality tier, insufficient by itself.

** Approach 2: resilient local search gateway (recommended baseline)

Combine persistent cache, quota-aware authorized APIs, self-hosted SearXNG,
best-effort direct routes, stale fallback, and explicit metadata.

Advantages:

- Pi retains full orchestration.
- Can target high non-empty-response availability.
- Uses scarce authorized API credits where they add the most value.
- Persistent state survives Pi processes and reports.
- Failures are explicit and measurable.

Disadvantages:

- Cannot guarantee freshness.
- SearXNG/upstream parsing remains operationally fragile.
- 99% availability depends materially on cached/stale/partial acceptance.
- More components and policy distinctions must be maintained.

Conclusion: best fit under the clarified requirements and $0 constraint.

** Approach 3: local crawler/index

Build or import a local searchable web corpus.

Advantages:

- Predictable local availability after indexing.
- No per-query provider quota for already indexed documents.

Disadvantages:

- A broad fresh web index has major storage, bandwidth, crawling, ranking, and
  compliance costs.
- Poor fit for a hard-$0 general-web freshness target.
- Estimated query reuse of 10--40% may not justify the initial complexity.

Conclusion: defer.  Reconsider only if observed query/domain reuse supports a
narrow, valuable local corpus.

* Recommended design baseline

This remains a proposal pending explicit approval.

** Components

1. *Gateway inside or adjacent to the web-search extension*
   - Receives normalized =web_lookup= requests.
   - Owns routing, deadlines, merge behavior, and response metadata.
2. *Persistent SQLite cache*
   - Shared across Pi sessions and processes.
   - Stores normalized query/parameters, results, timestamps, provider,
     freshness class, and quality/failure metadata.
3. *In-flight request coalescing*
   - Concurrent equivalent queries share one upstream request.
4. *Provider adapters*
   - Existing Exa, Tavily, and DuckDuckGo adapters.
   - Optional Brave, Bright Data, SearXNG, and other approved adapters.
5. *Quota ledger and rate limiter*
   - Persistent monthly counters plus in-memory token buckets for burst limits.
6. *Engine health manager*
   - Consecutive-failure counts, cooldowns, circuit breakers, and recovery
     probes.
7. *Merger/deduplicator*
   - Canonicalizes URLs, removes duplicates, preserves provenance, and merges
     non-identical useful snippets.
8. *Metrics and operator visibility*
   - Measures status, freshness, provider use, quota, latency, and failures.

** Routing sequence

#+BEGIN_SRC text
normalize query and parameters
        |
        v
fresh persistent cache hit? -- yes --> return status=fresh-cache
        |
        no
        v
coalesce with equivalent in-flight query if present
        |
        v
choose healthy provider with quota and rate tokens
        |
        +--> authorized/direct APIs
        +--> approved SERP APIs
        +--> self-hosted SearXNG / best-effort routes
        |
        v
merge + URL-deduplicate + persist
        |
        +--> non-empty live result: return status=fresh or partial
        |
        +--> live routes unavailable/empty
                    |
                    v
             stale cache hit? -- yes --> return status=stale
                    |
                    no
                    v
             explicit partial/unavailable response
#+END_SRC

The exact ordering within the live tier should be configurable and may depend
on query type, remaining monthly budget, historical quality, and latency.

** Query normalization and cache keys

At minimum, keys need to include:

- Whitespace and Unicode-normalized query text.
- Case normalization where semantics permit it.
- Requested engine or automatic routing mode.
- Result limit.
- Locale, country, language, safe-search, and time filters.
- Any search mode that materially changes results.
- A cache schema/version field so parser or ranking changes can invalidate old
  entries safely.

Do not normalize away quoted phrases, operators, punctuation, or filters that
change search meaning.

** Freshness model

Response states should distinguish at least:

- =fresh=: produced by a live upstream call within the request.
- =fresh-cache= or =cached=: cache entry within configured fresh TTL.
- =stale=: cache entry beyond fresh TTL but within allowed stale retention.
- =partial=: non-empty result set with one or more providers failed, timed out,
  or were skipped.
- =unavailable=: no non-empty live or cache result; should not be counted as an
  availability success.

Exact fresh TTLs and maximum stale ages were not approved during early
research.  They should be configurable by query/result class and selected from
observed reuse and freshness needs rather than invented globally.

Recommended metadata fields:

#+BEGIN_SRC json
{
  "status": "fresh|cached|stale|partial|unavailable",
  "query": "normalized query",
  "results": [],
  "providersAttempted": [],
  "providersSucceeded": [],
  "partialFailures": [],
  "fetchedAt": "timestamp",
  "cacheAgeSeconds": 0,
  "freshnessTtlSeconds": 0,
  "servedFromCache": false,
  "quotaSnapshot": {},
  "requestId": "..."
}
#+END_SRC

The public shape may need to preserve backward compatibility with the current
=web_lookup= tool while exposing additional metadata in a concise text or
structured envelope.

** Quota-aware scheduling

Do not implement "try providers until one emits 429" as normal scheduling.
Maintain local counters and reserve capacity across the remaining billing
period.

For a monthly allowance:

#+BEGIN_SRC text
safe_remaining = documented_remaining - reserve
pace_per_day = safe_remaining / days_remaining_in_provider_cycle
#+END_SRC

A weighted distribution over the 286/day broad total is approximately:

| Provider | Approximate share |
|----------+-------------------|
| Bright Data | 58.3% |
| Exa | 16.7% |
| Brave | 11.7% |
| Tavily basic | 11.7% |
| Serpstack | 1.2% |
| Zenserp | 0.6% |

Use those figures only after each provider is approved and configured.  A safer
operational allocation is approximately:

- Bright Data: 150/day.
- Exa: 42/day.
- Brave: 30/day.
- Tavily basic: 30/day.
- Serpstack: 3/day.
- Zenserp: 1/day.
- Total: approximately 256/day, with monthly reserve.

The scheduler should also support:

- Per-second/per-minute token buckets.
- Provider billing-cycle boundaries rather than assuming every month is exactly
  30 days.
- Atomic quota updates across concurrent Pi processes.
- Manual quota correction after dashboard reconciliation.
- Hard zero-cost behavior: never fall through to paid overage.
- Provider disable switches.
- Priority preservation for high-value or freshness-sensitive requests if the
  user later defines such classes.

** Cooldowns and circuit breakers

Suggested state transitions:

- Timeout/transient 5xx: short exponential backoff with jitter.
- =429= with =Retry-After=: disable until the indicated time.
- Repeated failures or parser-empty anomalies: open circuit for a longer
  cooldown.
- CAPTCHA/block page: suspend route; do not retry aggressively.
- Quota exhausted: disable until the next verified billing reset.
- Half-open recovery: one bounded probe, then close or reopen.

Track failures separately by engine and failure class.  An empty result can be
legitimate for a narrow query, so parser/block detection must not equate every
empty result with upstream failure.

** Result merging

- Normalize URLs by removing known tracking parameters and fragments where
  safe.
- Preserve canonical URL, original URL, provider, title, snippet, rank, and
  retrieval time.
- Deduplicate exact/canonical URLs across providers.
- Prefer more informative titles/snippets without discarding provenance.
- Do not pretend independently returned duplicates are independent evidence.
- Record which providers contributed each merged result.
- Keep provider-native ordering available for debugging.

** Observability

At minimum collect:

- Total calls and non-empty response rate.
- Counts by =fresh=, =cached=, =stale=, =partial=, and =unavailable=.
- Cache hit rate and cache-age distributions.
- Per-provider attempts, successes, empty responses, timeouts, blocks, 429s,
  latency, and result count.
- Quota remaining and projected exhaustion date.
- In-flight deduplication savings.
- URL overlap among providers.
- Percentage of requests requiring stale fallback.
- Percentage of partial responses and their failed providers.

These metrics are necessary to distinguish "the tool answered" from "the web
was searched freshly and comprehensively."

* Service objectives and honest claims

** Feasible target

A target such as the following is plausible only after measurement:

- 99% of calls return a non-empty response from live results, fresh cache, stale
  cache, or an explicitly partial non-empty result.
- Every response includes freshness and degradation metadata.
- Zero paid overage.
- No retry behavior that circumvents provider controls.

** Not supportable under these constraints

- 99% fresh general-web result availability at 5,000/day.
- 99% comprehensive coverage of the public web.
- A guarantee that SearXNG's famous-engine backends remain usable.
- A claim that 5,000 successful local SearXNG HTTP responses equal 5,000 fresh,
  independent search-index queries.
- A fixed number for Jina, DuckDuckGo HTML, or public SearXNG capacity without a
  published quota/SLA.

** Availability denominator

Before implementation, define precisely:

- Whether a partial response with one result counts.
- Whether an informative =unavailable= response counts only as tool uptime or
  as search success (recommended: tool uptime yes, search success no).
- Maximum acceptable stale age by use case.
- Whether duplicate-only results count.
- Whether a cache hit for exactly normalized parameters is required.

Report at least two objectives separately:

1. *Tool availability*: the function returns a valid response envelope.
2. *Search-result availability*: the response contains one or more results.

Also report live/fresh coverage separately so stale fallback cannot hide an
upstream outage.

* Rejected strategies and rationale

** Many Exa or Brave accounts

Rejected because it is quota multiplication rather than independent-provider
resilience.  Brave explicitly prohibits multiple-account limit bypass; Exa has
anti-circumvention and suspension language.  It also creates key-management,
identity, reset, and sudden-ban failure modes.

** Playwright/stealth automation of search engines

Rejected because browser mimicry does not create stable authorization or quota.
CAPTCHA and block thresholds are dynamic, browser maintenance is costly, and
forcing continued access would violate the no-circumvention requirement.

** Proxy rotation

Rejected under the $0 and compliance constraints.  Free proxies are unreliable
and unsafe; paid proxies violate the budget; rotation intended to evade blocks
is out of scope regardless of cost.

** Consumer research subscriptions as backends

Rejected because they do not grant Pi a raw high-volume search API.  They also
move query planning and evidence handling into another hosted agent.

** Claiming burst aggregation solves monthly capacity

Rejected mathematically.  Fifty requests/second is irrelevant once a provider's
1,000 monthly requests are consumed.  Burst limits control when requests may be
sent; credits control how many can be sent sustainably.

* Open decisions before implementation

- Approve or reject Approach 2 as the design baseline.
- Decide whether Bright Data and other third-party SERP scrapers are acceptable
  policy dependencies.  Without them, recurring numeric capacity falls from
  approximately 286/day to approximately 114/day.
- Decide which providers may require a payment card even though configured
  spend remains zero (notably Brave).
- Define fresh TTLs and maximum stale retention by query class.
- Define the exact success denominator for the 99% objective.
- Decide whether live providers race in parallel, run sequentially, or use a
  bounded hybrid.  Parallel racing improves latency but consumes multiple
  provider credits for one user query.
- Choose whether provider results are first-success or merged.  Merging improves
  coverage but multiplies quota use.
- Choose SQLite location, locking strategy, retention size, and privacy policy.
- Define cache handling for potentially sensitive queries.
- Decide whether explicit =engine= requests may fall back to other engines or
  must fail closed.
- Reconfirm every provider's quota, reset behavior, overage behavior, and terms
  immediately before implementation because free plans change frequently.
- Determine whether the extension should run SearXNG itself, call an externally
  managed local instance, or merely support an optional URL.
- Establish a test method using mocked provider responses; live tests must not
  consume uncontrolled quota.

* Research quality, contradictions, and gaps

- Provider pricing and free plans are volatile.  The values in this document
  are snapshots from official documentation reviewed during this research.
- "All available providers" cannot literally be exhaustive: scraper
  marketplaces, promotional credits, and small SERP vendors appear and vanish
  frequently.
- Search-result snippets sometimes disagreed with current official pages.  The
  conservative calculation counts only quantities that could be tied to
  official provider material, except where explicitly labeled reported or
  provisional.
- Bright Data's documentation maps its 5,000 monthly credits to successful SERP
  requests and says unlocking/CAPTCHA handling is included.  This supports the
  capacity calculation but also reinforces that it is a managed scraper, not
  first-party search-index authorization.
- ScraperAPI and Apify have real recurring free resources, but a stable
  query-equivalent requires actual configuration and billing telemetry.
- SerpAPI's recurring free quantity remained insufficiently verified from the
  official extracted page and is excluded from the conservative total.
- Jina's keyless access lacks a stable published hard allowance and is excluded
  from numeric capacity.
- SearXNG and DuckDuckGo can add substantial best-effort volume, but assigning a
  hard daily number would be false precision.
- A $0 system cannot make a contractual availability guarantee when its bulk
  live capacity comes from services with no SLA.

* Current recommendation

Adopt Approach 2 as the design baseline only if the availability goal is stated
honestly as high *non-empty response availability*, with separate reporting for
fresh/live coverage.  Use a persistent cache and quota-aware authorized APIs as
the reliable core, SearXNG and other no-SLA routes as best-effort enrichment,
and stale/partial responses as explicit degradation modes.

Do not claim that this architecture produces 5,000 fresh searches/day at $0.
It produces up to 5,000 Pi-controlled lookup attempts/day and seeks to answer
most of them through a measured combination of live search, cache, stale data,
and partial results.
